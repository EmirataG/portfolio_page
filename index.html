Robotics

Mars Rover, Yale Undergraduate Robotics

Communications & Control Subteam Lead · Sept 2026 – present

I lead wireless communications, telemetry and teleoperation for Yale's entry in the University Rover Challenge 2027.

  • Designed the rover-to-base radio architecture: a 2.4 GHz Ubiquiti main link with a 915 MHz backup, modeled on top teams' published designs and fitted to URC band rules and FCC limits. Specified and procured the full RF system for about $590.
  • Built a ROS 2 bridge that carries commands and telemetry over a low-bandwidth serial radio, with packet framing, CRC error checking and rate limiting.
  • Set up the rover and base-station Raspberry Pi 5s on Ubuntu and ROS 2 Jazzy, with secure remote access for the team.
  • Evaluated perception and navigation hardware (stereo depth camera, NVIDIA Jetson, end-effector camera, RTK GNSS) against cost, rules and competition evidence.

Software

Manuscript

Creator and sole developer · 2026

A web app that turns a music score and a recording into a synced score-animation video, with every note lighting up as it sounds.

  • Rendering runs entirely in the browser: Verovio (WASM) engraving, a beat-domain timing core with user-placed sync anchors, and MP4 export up to 4K through WebCodecs.
  • An automatic audio-to-score aligner proposes sync anchors from a real performance; pitch-preserving slow-motion scrubbing handles manual correction.
  • GPU-warped previews (perspective and curved staves), progressive note reveal, and layered highlights that stay pixel-aligned between live preview and export.
  • Shipped end to end on Next.js, TypeScript and Firebase: direct-to-storage uploads, versioned media delivery with browser-side caching, autosave with undo/redo, and a project dashboard.
  • A performance and parity harness plus unit and integration tests (Vitest, real Verovio fixtures) keep preview and export frame-identical.

See example videos on the YouTube channel.

Hardware

PosturePoke

5th place, YHack 2026 Hardware Track · March 2026

A wearable posture monitor, built with a team, that clips to your collar and escalates from beeps to an AI voice coach to a servo-powered poke when you slouch.

  • Streams IMU pitch and roll at 10 Hz from an Arduino straight to the browser over the Web Serial API, with no server in between, and calibrates per user.
  • Custom coaches are generated from a text description: Gemini writes the in-character scripts and ElevenLabs designs the voice and speaks them.
  • A social layer on Firebase adds friends, shared coaches and session history charts.

Pec Synth

A wearable instrument played by flexing the pecs

Two IMU boards (XIAO nRF52840, Arduino C++) stream motion to a Python host on a Raspberry Pi, which detects flexes, maps them to melodies in Turkish maqams and synthesizes the audio.

  • Tuned detection against a recorded, labeled session to 53 of 54 flexes.
  • Cut flex-to-sound latency from about 300 ms to under 100 ms.
  • Built an ESP32-S3 control box and a systemd service so it starts on boot.

Art

Faking Art

Generative painting for a public LED display

A p5.js piece in which splashes of color from a painter's palette are pulled toward a hidden gravity point, can't cross each other's traces, and grow until they fill the canvas. It asks whether palette and spatial bias alone are enough to make randomness read as intentional. Runs unattended on a Raspberry Pi.
